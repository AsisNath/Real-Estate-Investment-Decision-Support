## 1. Short-Term Rental (STR) Rules — ⚠ HIGH ATTENTION

- **A permit is required for every STR unit, and STR means any stay under 30 consecutive days.** Chapter 852 of the Indianapolis–Marion County Code establishes the permit program, administered by the Department of Business & Neighborhood Services (DBNS). — [Municode, Indianapolis–Marion County Ch. 852](https://library.municode.com/in/indianapolis_-_marion_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TITIVBUCORELI_CH852INSHRMREPEPR_S852-108PEEN) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary (official ordinance page returned HTTP 403 to automated access; confirm text with DBNS at 317-327-8700)
- **Applications are filed entirely online** through the DBNS Citizen Access (Accela) portal at `accela9ca.indy.gov/citizenaccess/`; an account must be created first, and DBNS aims to review complete applications within about 10 business days. — [City of Indianapolis DBNS, Short-Term Rental User Guide (Dec 2024)](https://us-east-1-indy.graphassets.com/ActDBC5rvRWeCZlNNnLrDz/EplHYj6SQn6hKWwKpxgc) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official (city-published PDF, retrieved and read)
- **Fee reported as $150 per unit, one time, with annual renewal and no renewal fee** (Indiana law is reported to bar a renewal fee). Underwrite the permit as an annual compliance obligation, not a recurring cost. — [Municode Ch. 852](https://library.municode.com/in/indianapolis_-_marion_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TITIVBUCORELI_CH852INSHRMREPEPR_S852-108PEEN) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary; **confirm the current fee and renewal terms with DBNS before modeling STR income**
- **Reported operating conditions:** a designated local contact reachable 24/7, maximum two guests per bedroom, minimum $500,000 liability insurance, and a possible inspection for building-code and habitability compliance. The unit must be a legally constructed dwelling. — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary; confirm each with DBNS
- **Unverified and material to an investor:** whether Chapter 852 imposes a primary-residence or owner-occupancy condition. Several jurisdictions do, and it would decide whether a non-owner-occupied investment unit can operate as an STR at all. The ordinance text could not be read directly. **Confirm with DBNS before assuming STR is available for a non-owner-occupied property.**

## 2. Landlord–Tenant Law (State of Indiana)

- **No statutory cap on the security deposit amount.** Indiana does not set a maximum, unlike many states. — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary; confirm against IC 32-31-3
- **Return deadline: 45 days after the tenancy ends**, by mailing an itemized list of damages claimed plus a check for the remainder of the deposit. — [IC 32-31-3-12 / 32-31-3-14 via Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-32/article-31/chapter-3/section-32-31-3-12/) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary (iga.in.gov returned no statutory text to automated retrieval; verify at iga.in.gov or with counsel)
- **Missing the 45-day deadline or omitting the itemized list voids the landlord's right to retain any part of the deposit**, and the tenant may recover the full deposit plus reasonable attorney's fees. This is a harsher penalty than a simple refund — a calendar discipline issue, not a rounding error. — [IC 32-31-3-12 via Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-32/article-31/chapter-3/section-32-31-3-12/) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary; verify with counsel
- **Permitted deductions** are unpaid rent, unpaid utility or sewer charges, and damage from the tenant's noncompliance with the rental agreement. — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary

## 3. Rent Control — INFO (positive for the investor)

- **Indiana prohibits local rent control.** IC 32-31-1-20(b) bars units of local government from regulating rental rates on privately owned property, by zoning ordinance or otherwise, unless the General Assembly authorizes it. The same section also bars local regulation of much of the landlord–tenant relationship, including tenant screening criteria. — [IC 32-31-1-20 via Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-32/article-31/chapter-1/section-32-31-1-20/) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary (official iga.in.gov text not retrievable; the statutory citation itself is consistent across sources)
- **Exception worth knowing:** the preemption does not apply to property receiving federal, state, or local funds specifically to provide reduced rents to low- or moderate-income tenants. Irrelevant to a market-rate purchase, decisive if the property carries an affordability covenant. — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary
- **Investor implication:** rent growth here is constrained by the market, not by law. Marion County has no rent stabilization regime.

## 4. HOA / Deed Restrictions — ⚠ VERIFY PER-PROPERTY

- **Indiana HOAs can restrict or cap rentals, and can adopt such a restriction after you buy.** Authority comes from the recorded declaration (CC&Rs) plus state law; a properly noticed board meeting with sufficient votes can impose limits. — [Indiana HOA law overview](https://www.hopb.co/indiana) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary
- **Governing statute:** the Indiana Homeowners Associations Act, IC 32-25.5, governs associations formed after June 30, 2009; condominiums fall under the Indiana Condominium Act, IC 32-25. — [Indiana Homeowners Associations Act, IC 32-25.5](https://www.hopb.co/indiana-homeowners-associations-act-title-32-article-255) — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ secondary
- **Unverified for this specific property:** whether 46202 parcels carry HOA, condo, or deed rental restrictions, and whether any cap is already at its limit. Downtown and near-north Indianapolis includes many condo conversions where this is decisive. **Obtain the recorded declaration, bylaws, and any amendments from Marion County records, and ask the association in writing whether a rental cap exists and how many slots remain.**
