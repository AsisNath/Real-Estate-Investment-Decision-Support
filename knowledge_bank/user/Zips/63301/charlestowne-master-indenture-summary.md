## 1. Leasing and Rental Restrictions — the decisive finding

- **Article X contains no leasing restriction of any kind.** The 17 enumerated restrictions are: Building Use, Building Size and Location, Resubdivision, Commercial Use, Nuisances, Obstructions, Animals, Trucks/Boats, Abandoned Vehicles, Vehicular Sight Lines, Out Buildings, Fences, Signs, Utility and Drainage Easements, Oil Drilling, Cul-De-Sac, and Hazardous Materials. **There is no rental cap, no minimum lease term, no owner-occupancy requirement, and no short-term-rental ban.** — Master Indenture, Article X (Bk DE4386 Pg 1610–1612) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official (recorded instrument, read directly)
- **The one clause that could be turned against a short-term rental is Article X.4, Commercial Use:** "no commercial activities of any kind shall be conducted on any Lot or in any Living Unit," excepting the Grantor's promotional activity and "the conduct of a home occupation in strict accordance with the provisions of the applicable zoning ordinances." Conventional long-term residential leasing is not normally treated as a commercial activity *conducted on the Lot*; nightly or weekly rental is materially more arguable, and associations elsewhere have used clauses worded like this to challenge STRs. **Treat STR here as legally untested, not as permitted.** — Article X.4 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1610) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **A "For Rent" sign is not permitted on a literal reading.** Article X.13 allows only a "for sale" sign no larger than 18" × 24"; no other "signs, advertisements, billboards or advertising structures of any kind" may be erected or displayed on any Lot. Marketing a rental has to happen online or through an agent, not curbside. — Article X.13 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1612) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Article X.1, Building Use** requires Director approval before any building is "used for a purpose other than that for which the building or structure was originally designed" — i.e. residential. A residential tenancy does not change the design purpose; a converted or commercial use would. — Article X.1 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1610) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official

## 2. Can a rental restriction be added later? — meaningful investor protection

- **Adding a leasing restriction is expressly a "material" amendment requiring mortgagee consent.** Article XI.4(b) lists the subjects that cannot be materially amended, or newly added, without the consent of at least **67% of Class "A" Members** and of Grantor (while it owns land subject to the Indenture), plus approval of Eligible Holders of first Mortgages on Lots to which at least **51% of the votes of mortgaged Lots** appertain. Item **(ix) on that list is "leasing of Lots."** — Article XI.4(b)(ix) (Bk DE4386 Pg 1614–1615) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Why this matters:** in many associations a simple board vote can impose a rental cap after you buy, which is the single largest HOA risk to a rental strategy. Here it takes a supermajority of owners *and* the first mortgagees. That is a high bar, and it is a genuine protection — not an absolute one, but far better than the Missouri default.
- **General amendment rule:** until all Lots authorized for development have been sold and conveyed for residential use, **only the Grantor may amend**. After that, amendment requires the written consent of **two-thirds of all Owners**, and must be recorded with the County Recorder of Deeds to take effect. — Article XII.4 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1617) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Term:** the covenants run with and bind the land for **30 years from recordation — through 2036-01-04** — then automatically renew for successive 10-year periods unless owners of two-thirds of the Lots record a termination instrument at least one year in advance, with 90 days' written notice to every Owner. These restrictions will outlast any normal hold period. — Article XII.9 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1618) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official

## 3. Costs an investor must underwrite

- **Initial capital contribution: a one-time charge equal to 2× the annual assessment fee per Lot,** assessed and collected at the initial Owner's closing. Confirm with the Association whether this is re-triggered on resale — the clause reads as applying to "the initial Owner." — Article IX.12 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1610) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **$100 transfer and administrative fee on every conveyance** other than by Grantor, payable to the Directors with written notice of the conveyance. Unpaid, it becomes a lien on the transferred Lot. Budget it as a closing cost on both purchase and eventual sale. — Article IX.13 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1610) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Delinquent assessments bear interest at 1% over the Prime Rate** from the date of delinquency, and become a lien on the Lot together with costs of collection. — Article IX.8 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1609) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **The assessment lien is subordinate to an institutional first mortgage** recorded before the assessments came due. Foreclosure does not, however, relieve the Lot of assessments falling due afterward. — Article IX.8 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1609) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Enforcement costs run against the violator:** the Association may recover damages plus reasonable attorneys' fees and court costs in any enforcement proceeding. A covenant dispute is not a cost-free disagreement. — Article XII.1 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1616) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **The annual assessment amount itself is set under Article IX.3 and is not fixed in this instrument.** Get the current annual assessment, the reserve balance, and any pending special assessment from the Association in writing before closing. — as of 2026-08-01 ⚠ not stated in the recorded document

## 4. Operating restrictions that shape the tenant pool

- **Pets: no more than two dogs, cats, or other household pets** per Lot or Living Unit, none kept "for any commercial purpose," all leashed at all times, and no runs or outside pet structures. Pets with vicious propensities are excluded outright, and the Directors may declare a noisy pet a prohibited nuisance in their sole judgment. This narrows the pet-friendly premium some rental strategies rely on. — Article X.7 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1611) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Vehicle storage is tightly limited.** No buses, trucks over 3/4 ton, commercial vehicles, boats, motorcycles, RVs, campers, or trailers may be parked or stored on any Lot unless inside an enclosed garage. No vehicle repair or servicing in front of or adjacent to any residence. Relevant if the likely tenant is a tradesperson with a work vehicle. — Article X.8 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1611) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **No sheds, trailers, tents, or out buildings of any kind at any time,** and no permanent basketball or sports goals anywhere in the Subdivision. — Article X.11 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1611) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **Fences: wrought iron, ornamental metal, or vinyl only,** with placement rules that differ between Village A (townhomes) and Villages B and C (single family). Village A permits fencing only in narrowly defined situations. — Article X.12 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1612) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official
- **No exterior lighting may be directed outside the boundaries of a Lot,** and nothing may be stored, hung, or exposed on the Common Ground or on any Lot exterior — including laundry. — Article X.5, X.6 (Bk DE4386 Pg 1610–1611) — as of 2026-08-01 ✅ official

## 6. What to do before closing

- **Obtain the Subindenture for the specific Village** containing the subject Lot, from the Association or St. Charles County Recorder of Deeds. This is the single largest open item — it can reverse the central finding above.
- **Run a recorder search for amendments** to Book DE4386 Page 1586 filed since 2006-01-04, and for any separately recorded rules or resolutions.
- **Ask the Charlestowne Master Association in writing** whether any rental, lease-term, or short-term-rental restriction exists today, whether any is under consideration, and what the current annual assessment and reserve position are. Get the answer in writing.
- **Confirm the Lot is within Charlestowne Plat 1** by matching the legal description in Exhibit A to the property's own legal description on the title commitment.
- **Have a Missouri real estate attorney read Article X.4 against the intended rental strategy** if short-term rental is part of the underwriting.
